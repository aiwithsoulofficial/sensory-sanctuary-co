Our story

We built what we couldn't find

Rachel lives with anxiety. As a model, she's expected to look calm, composed, in control. What most people don't see is the racing heart, the overwhelm in loud rooms, the moments where everything is too much. She needed something discreet, something beautiful, something that worked. She couldn't find it.

Caitlin is an Occupational Therapist. She spent years recommending sensory tools to clients - but nobody wanted to use them in public because they looked clinical, childish, or obvious. The gap between what works therapeutically and what people will wear in public was massive.

The science

Why tactile input helps your nervous system regulate

This isn't a trend. Sensory jewellery is grounded in decades of occupational therapy research on sensory processing and nervous system regulation.

01

Tactile input grounds the nervous system

Repetitive tactile stimulation signals safety to your autonomic nervous system. This is why fidgeting naturally calms anxiety.

02

Proprioceptive feedback supports focus

The gentle resistance of a spinner ring provides proprioceptive input - the deep pressure sense that helps your brain organise and focus.

03

Self-regulation is a skill

OTs know that regulation isn't about "being calm" - it's about having tools to shift your state. A sensory ring is a portable tool you can access anywhere.
